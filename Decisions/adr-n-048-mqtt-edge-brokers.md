# ADR-N-048: MQTT Edge Brokers per Project, RMQTT as Is, Keycloak Tokens Checked by the Gateway

Date: 2026-10-12  
Status: Accepted  
Amended: 2026-10-20, items 9 to 12 of section 2 (T-4858); 2026-10-25, items 1 and 4 (T-5561)

Amendment: product owner, 2026-10-20 ~19:20Z ("only admin can assign one mqtt with certification to the project, and then user can add and there is limit of mqtt brokers"; "just mqtt inbound without actually having values"); the choices were delegated and taken by the supervisor and worker-t4858 (owner may override)

## 1. Context

The owner, 2026-10-11: a Rust MQTT server with ACL, published on ports of its own so nothing collides, holding no state, with a Bento pipeline carrying its messages into the context broker. Cameras publishing to an address are the first use. In a second order the same evening: "there is already a Rust MQTT broker, just use that". A project may run **several** brokers, and each one is a manifest in the project's Git repository like every other kind ([Architecture/06](../Architecture/06-configuration-as-code.md)).

What exists today: a `DataSource` of `type: mqtt` (MF-35, PL-39) subscribes a pipeline to an **external** broker, with a password from a `secretRef`. Nothing in the platform accepts an MQTT connection itself.

The other half of the path is the pipeline, and it has one constraint. Bento's `mqtt` input reads its `password` once, as a plain string ([internal/impl/mqtt/client.go](https://github.com/warpstreamlabs/bento/blob/main/internal/impl/mqtt/client.go)). A Keycloak access token expires within minutes, and every reconnect would present the stale one. The pinned runner is upstream Bento, which we do not fork.

## 2. Decision

A project declares **MQTT edge brokers** as manifests, `kind: MqttBroker`. Each one is a release of its own of the unmodified RMQTT image. Devices authenticate with a Keycloak token that the **context gateway** checks for the broker, and a pipeline reads the broker on an internal listener that only the pipeline runner can reach.

1. **The kind.** `MqttBroker` lives in a project at `projects/{p}/mqttbrokers/{name}.yaml` and is served at `/api/v1/projects/{project}/mqttbrokers` (MF-11). Its name is a DNS label, unique in the project. `spec` holds:
   - `roles`: a list of `{name, publish, subscribe, sharedTopics, reason}`: what each role may do. `publish` and `subscribe` are topic filters that may use `${clientid}`. Who holds a role is written on the ServiceAccount (item 13); the older `serviceAccounts` list on a role is deprecated and still read for one release.
   - `limits`: `maxMessageBytes` (default 65536, at most 1 MiB), `messagesPerSecond` per client (default 10, at most 1000), `maxConnections` (default 1000, at most 10000).
   - `port`: optional, see item 5.
   - `mode`, `address`, `hostname` and `deviceAuth`: see items 10 to 12.

   A device role may hold `subscribe` filters only under its own `${clientid}`, and only in `relay` mode (item 10). The reconciler refuses a filter that is `#` or begins with `$`.

   Every `publish` filter of a role holds a `${clientid}` level before any wildcard, so a device publishes only under its own id (amendment 2026-10-25, T-5561): `cameras/+/#` would let each camera of the role publish as any other. A role whose devices do share topics says so with `sharedTopics: true`; the filters are then taken as written. Every `MqttBroker` change already takes the red lane, so a person with `approve` decides each such role.
2. **The engine, as is.** RMQTT 0.24.0, pinned by the digest above and mirrored and signed by the deployment like every other third-party image. We write no broker code, only configuration: listener limits from `spec.limits`, `max_qos_allowed = 1`, `max_session_expiry_interval = 0`, no retainer, no session storage, no message storage, `rmqtt-auth-http` pointed at the gateway with `deny_if_error = true` and `disconnect_if_expiry = true`, `http_acl_req` pointed at the gateway's `POST /internal/mqtt/acl` for each publish topic (§2.3), and `rmqtt-acl` holding the one rule `["deny", "all"]`. Clean sessions only, QoS 0 and 1 kept in memory: a restart loses the messages in flight and nothing else.
3. **Devices: a Keycloak token, checked by the gateway.** A camera is a `ServiceAccount` (PF-45, PF-47). It connects with the client id and username of its Keycloak client `{project}-{sa}` and an access token as the password. RMQTT's own JWT plugin would need the realm key as a static PEM and could not follow a key rotation, so the token is checked where tokens are already checked: `rmqtt-auth-http` posts the CONNECT to the gateway's internal route `POST /internal/mqtt/auth`. The gateway allows the connection only when every check below holds:
   - the token verifies against the realm JWKS;
   - its audience holds the broker's client `mqtt-{project}-{name}`;
   - its `azp` equals both the username and the client id;
   - the account holds a role of this broker.

   The device gets that token by its own key pair: its account's `oauth-client` credential carries the public JWKS, and the reconciler writes the client `{project}-{sa}` with `client-jwt` authentication. The device signs a client assertion and holds no secret ([Architecture/12 §3](../Architecture/12-identity-and-access.md#a-device-by-its-own-key-pair-pf-47-t-4015), T-4015).

   A token past its `exp` is refused with no leeway, and so is an account whose credentials have all passed their `expiresAt`. The answer carries the role's `subscribe` filters as the RMQTT `acl` list (`qos: [0, 1]`, empty when the role subscribes to nothing, since without a list the broker forgets `expire_at`), `superuser: false`, and `expire_at` set to the token's `exp` or the latest credential `expiresAt`, whichever comes first, so the broker drops the connection then and the device reconnects with a new token. A publish matches none of those rules, so the broker posts each publish topic to `POST /internal/mqtt/acl`, which decides it against the repository as it is now: the account still exists, still holds a role of the broker whose `publish` filter covers the topic with `${clientid}` bound, and still has a credential in force. The gateway also holds each client to its broker's `messagesPerSecond`, a token bucket per broker and client id that holds and refills that many per second, since RMQTT 0.24 limits only delivery to a subscriber (`mqueue_rate_limit`) and has no per-publisher rate. The answer carries no `X-Cache`, so the broker asks for every publish, and it disconnects a client whose publish is refused: removing a device's role or account ends its publishing at its next publish instead of at its token's `exp`, an hour on dev (T-5560), and a device above its rate is disconnected at the first publish over it (T-5562). A retained publish stores nothing, since no retainer runs. Subscriptions stay fixed at CONNECT and end with the session. The gateway learns which broker is asking from the caller's mesh identity (Linkerd `l5d-client-id`, the broker's Kubernetes ServiceAccount `mqtt-{project}-{name}`), never from a request field, so no broker can ask with another broker's ACL. Only the broker pods reach the route; it is not on APISIX.
4. **Pipelines: the internal listener, by mesh identity.** A `DataSource` of `type: mqtt` may name a broker of its own project with `spec.mqtt.brokerRef: {kind: MqttBroker, name}` in place of `urls`. The reconciler renders the broker's internal Service URL on port 1883 and, as the username, the runner's name for the project, `pipeline-runner-{project}` (amendment 2026-10-25, T-5561). The DataSource carries no password and no `passwordRef`. The internal listener admits the runner's Kubernetes ServiceAccount alone: a Linkerd `Server` with an `AuthorizationPolicy` (MeshTLSAuthentication) and a NetworkPolicy from the runner namespace. The gateway allows a CONNECT without a password only for that username, the one of the broker's own project, and only from the address of one of the runner's pods: it resolves the runner's headless Service (`pipeline-runner-pods`, `JC_GATEWAY_MQTT_RUNNER_HOST`) per such CONNECT. Every connection to the public listener arrives through Traefik with the PROXY protocol, so its `ipaddr` is the client's own address, public, or a pod's for a cluster workload that dials the public host. Neither is a runner pod's, and such a CONNECT is refused there (T-4004: a private address alone let any cluster pod pass as the runner). The answer's `acl` grants `subscribe` on the topic filters of the DataSources that reference this broker and nothing else, with no `publish` and no `expire_at`. A DataSource or a Pipeline never names a platform broker by URL, a host whose first label starts with `mqtt-` written as a cluster Service name (`mqtt-…`, `mqtt-….{namespace}`, `mqtt-….{namespace}.svc…`), and never a username that starts with `pipeline-runner`: it reaches a broker of its own project by `brokerRef` and no other (T-5561). Without those two refusals a DataSource of project A could have named B's broker with B's runner name and read B's device stream. What remains is the ceiling the shared runner already sets for the token service ([Architecture/12 §One principal per pipeline](../Architecture/12-identity-and-access.md)): a stream on the runner can name what another stream may read. One runner per project lifts it later. The pipeline writes onward as every pipeline does, through an Endpoint and its Policy (CC-61).
5. **Addresses.** The default is TLS on 8883 through a Traefik TCP router matching `HostSNI(<name>.<project>.mqtt.<domain>)`. Traefik terminates TLS with a certificate cert-manager issues for that one hostname by HTTP-01, never a wildcard, and forwards plain TCP with the PROXY protocol to the broker's external listener. One port serves every broker and nothing collides. The hostname is derived from the name and the project, so it is unique across the platform by construction. `port: <n>` asks for a dedicated port from the reserved range 18830–18849, and only a port an org admin assigned to the project (item 11): the reconciler refuses a port another broker holds, naming that broker. A dedicated port is a Traefik entrypoint `mqtt-<port>` of its own, whose one route matches `HostSNI(*)` and passes TLS through: Traefik answers a client that sends no SNI with its one default certificate, never a route's (T-5856, measured on Traefik 3.5), so RMQTT terminates TLS itself on a listener 8884 with the broker host's certificate, and Traefik writes the PROXY protocol v2 header before the TLS bytes, which RMQTT reads first. A device without SNI connects and keeps its address. RMQTT reads the certificate once at start: the pod carries the Certificate's `status.revision` as an annotation, so a renewal rolls the broker at the next reconcile. The edge serves 18830–18839; 18840–18849 stay reserved, and a broker given one of them is not applied, its reason naming the served range (T-5856). A broker has one outside door: its dedicated port when `port` is set, else 8883 by hostname, never both. A broker with a port gets no 8883 route and no plain listener 1884; a pool port no broker holds keeps its entrypoint with no route, so Traefik closes the connection, and the firewall opens the served range as one rule (T-5871). Plain 1883 is never published outside the cluster.
6. **One release per broker.** Each broker has its own Keycloak client `mqtt-{project}-{name}` (the audience, no secret, bearer-only), its own Deployment, ConfigMap, Service and Kubernetes ServiceAccount in `{instance}-mqtt`, and its own metrics. Two brokers of one project share no client, no ACL and no credential, and a token minted for one broker's audience is refused by every other.
7. **Configuration as code.** The Portal edits the manifest through a commit, in the review lane where the project uses one. `jcctl apply` and a hand-edited file in Git do the same. The reconciler renders the release, the Keycloak client and the Traefik route, and deletes all three with the manifest. Secrets stay `secretRef` only: a broker needs none of its own.
8. **Measured by the Portal, read-only.** Each broker starts RMQTT's `rmqtt-http-api` on 6060 with no token, only where the instance runs the service mesh, and nothing else may call it: a Linkerd `Server` on 6060 admits the Portal's Kubernetes ServiceAccount alone (MeshTLSAuthentication), and an `HTTPRoute` beside it routes only `GET /api/v1/metrics/history/sum` and `GET /api/v1/clients`, so the API's publish, subscribe and kick routes answer nobody. A NetworkPolicy lets the Portal's pods reach 6060 and nothing else. Without the mesh the plugin stays off and the Portal shows "not measured" ([API/01 §44](../API/01-portal-api.md), T-4043).
9. **An allowance per project, set by an org admin (amendment 2026-10-20).** MQTT is off for every project until an org admin turns it on. The switch and the limits are one entry of the organization limits catalog ([ADR-N-035](adr-n-035-organization-policies-and-limits.md)): `Organization.spec.limits.mqtt` is the allowance every project gets, and `Project.spec.quotas.mqtt` replaces it for one project as a whole object. The catalog default is `enabled: false`. The fields:

   | Field | Meaning | Default once `enabled: true` | Built-in bound |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `enabled` | the project may declare brokers at all | `false` | — |
   | `brokers` | how many `MqttBroker` manifests the project may hold | 1 | 20 |
   | `connections` | the sum of `spec.limits.maxConnections` over the project's brokers | 1000 | 20000 |
   | `messagesPerSecond` | the sum over the project's brokers of `maxConnections` × `messagesPerSecond`, the most the brokers may accept in one second | 10000, one broker at its own defaults (1000 connections × 10) | 100000 |
   | `maxMessageBytes` | the ceiling of each broker's `spec.limits.maxMessageBytes` | 65536 | 1048576 |
   | `modes` | the broker modes the project may use (item 10) | `[ingest]` | `ingest`, `capture`, `relay` |
   | `addresses` | the address kinds the project may use (item 11) | `[platform]` | `platform`, `dedicatedPort`, `customHostname` |
   | `ports` | dedicated ports from 18830–18849 assigned to this project | `[]` | each port held by one project |
   | `customHostnames` | `{name, certificateRef}` entries the project may use (item 11) | `[]` | 10 |
   | `deviceAuth` | how devices may authenticate (item 12) | `[token]` | `token`, `clientCertificate` |
   | `clientCaRef` | the secretRef of the project's device client CA (item 12) | none | required when `deviceAuth` holds `clientCertificate` |

   The bounds are the operator's (`portal.organizationBounds`), each at most the built-in bound and the MF-54 ceilings. Who writes what:
   - Only an org admin changes `spec.limits.mqtt` or a project's `spec.quotas.mqtt`. Both are red-lane Changes an `org-admin` approves (PF-98). A Change that raises any value, adds a mode, an address kind, a port, a hostname or a device authentication needs the approval of an org admin other than the one who proposed it (four-eyes, T-4833). A project role never writes either field, whatever its verbs on `Project`.
   - Unlike the other project overrides of the catalog, `spec.quotas.mqtt` may exceed the organization's value: the organization's value is the default, and enabling one project while the rest stay off is the common case. The operator's bound is the ceiling for both.
   - Inside the allowance, a project member with `propose` on `MqttBroker` adds, edits and deletes brokers as today (item 7). Nobody asks an admin per broker.

   The Portal's verdict refuses, on every door before a `Change` exists, a broker that would exceed what is left, and the reconciler checks it again before it renders. The refusal names the limit, its value, the usage and who can raise it (PF-101), for example "the project may run 2 MQTT brokers (2 in use); an org admin can raise this in Organization settings > Projects > helsinki". A lowered allowance refuses the next broker and stops none that runs (PF-100). Disabling MQTT for a project refuses every new broker and every edit that is not a deletion; running brokers keep running until a Change deletes them, and the project's usage shows them over the allowance.
10. **Broker modes.** `spec.mode` says what the broker does with a message. A mode outside the allowance's `modes` is refused.
    - `ingest` (the default): devices publish and nothing else. No device role holds `subscribe` filters, the only subscriber is the project's pipeline runner on the internal listener (item 4), and the broker keeps nothing. With no DataSource on the broker, a message reaches nobody and is dropped: a project can run MQTT inbound before it has a model or a pipeline. The Portal still shows the connected devices and the message rate (item 8).
    - `capture`: `ingest`, plus the Portal keeps the newest messages so a steward can design a mapping before a model exists. `spec.capture` holds `messagesPerTopic` (default 20, at most 1000) and `window` (an ISO 8601 duration, default `PT1H`, at most `PT24H`). The Portal reads the broker on its internal listener by its own mesh identity, admitted like the runner's (item 4) with `subscribe` on the publish filters of the broker's roles and nothing else. It keeps at most `messagesPerTopic` messages per topic, 1000 messages and 8 MiB per broker, each for `window` from its arrival, in the Portal's database, and deletes them when the window passes, when the broker leaves `capture`, and when the broker is deleted. The captured messages are served only to a person whose binding holds `approve` on `MqttBroker` in the project (the steward, the project administrator, an org admin); a person who reads the manifest and holds no `approve` gets `404`, the same answer as a broker that does not exist. A steward may delete the capture at once. A payload is never written to a log, an activity entry or a metric.
    - `relay`: devices may also subscribe, each only under its own `${clientid}` prefix (item 1), as before this amendment. A manifest that holds a device `subscribe` filter and sets no `mode` is refused with a message that names `spec.mode: relay`, so no broker changes behaviour silently.
11. **Addresses and server certificates.** `spec.address` picks one kind, from the allowance's `addresses`:
    - `platform` (the default): `<name>.<project>.mqtt.<domain>` on 8883, its certificate issued for that hostname by HTTP-01 (item 5).
    - `dedicatedPort`: `spec.port` names one of the ports in the allowance's `ports`. An org admin assigns a port to one project; a project member only picks among its own. The reconciler refuses a port assigned to no project or to another project, and a port another broker of the project holds, naming that broker. A port is never claimed by a user: there are 20, and the allowance is how they are shared.
    - `customHostname`: `spec.hostname` names one entry of the allowance's `customHostnames`, a name the city owns (`mqtt.city.example`). The city points a CNAME at the edge; the Traefik route matches `HostSNI` on that name. With `certificateRef` empty, cert-manager issues the certificate by HTTP-01 once the name resolves to the edge, and the Portal shows the broker as waiting for DNS until then. With `certificateRef` set, the edge serves the certificate the org admin stored there (a `secretRef`, never inline), and the Portal shows its expiry date and warns 30 days before it.
    A hostname is assigned to one project at a time: the Portal refuses a `customHostnames` entry another project's allowance holds, naming that project.
12. **Devices by client certificate.** Some devices cannot run an OAuth flow. When the allowance's `deviceAuth` holds `clientCertificate` and the broker's `spec.deviceAuth` names it, the broker's external listener asks for a client certificate signed by the project's client CA (`clientCaRef`, uploaded by the org admin as a secret, never inline). TLS then terminates in RMQTT for that broker, on a dedicated port or behind a TCP passthrough route on 8883, so the certificate reaches the broker; Traefik does not see it. The certificate's subject common name is the device's ServiceAccount name, the gateway receives it as the CONNECT username with no password, and the gateway allows the CONNECT only when that ServiceAccount holds a role of the broker, the certificate is within its validity and not on the CRL the CA secret carries, and the client id equals `{project}-{serviceAccount}`. The ACL and the role checks are the same as for a token, and `expire_at` is one hour after the CONNECT or the certificate's `notAfter`, whichever comes first, so the device reconnects every hour and each reconnect is checked again. Revoking a device means listing its certificate on the CRL or removing its ServiceAccount from the role; an open connection ends within the hour. A broker without `clientCertificate` asks for no client certificate, and a token is still accepted on a broker that names both.
13. **Who holds a role (T-5872).** The broker says what each role may do; the ServiceAccount says who holds it. A device fleet gets a role by a RoleBinding on its ServiceAccount, `role: mqtt/<broker>/<role>` or `role: mqtt/*/<role>` (every broker of the project that declares `<role>`), with `scope.project` the account's own project and an optional `validity`. Adding, expiring or revoking a fleet is then a ServiceAccount change, reviewed and approved like any other grant (AG-131), and the broker manifest stays untouched.
    - **Validation.** An `mqtt/` binding names a broker by DNS label or `*` and a role by DNS label, is scoped to `project` only, and that project is the account's own; it carries no `types` or `operations`. A binding naming another project is refused by `jcctl validate` and the Portal, and the gateway grants nothing for it at CONNECT.
    - **Decision.** One function in jc-core, `mqtt::roles_held`, answers which roles of one broker an account holds at one instant: the roles whose name a binding in force names (with this broker or `*`), unioned with the deprecated `serviceAccounts` lists. The gateway's CONNECT and publish checks call it, the Portal's explain route calls it, and nothing else decides it. No binding and no legacy entry means no role, and no role means the CONNECT is refused. A role the broker stops declaring grants nothing, whatever the bindings say.
    - **One topic validator.** jc-core `mqtt::topics` judges every filter, called by `jcctl validate`, the Portal reconciler and form, and the gateway: no bare `#` and no filter of wildcards alone (`+/#`, `+`), no `$` topic, at most 8 levels and 128 bytes, no empty level, no NUL; a `publish` filter holds `${clientid}` before any wildcard unless the role sets `sharedTopics: true`, which needs a `reason`; `subscribe` only on a `relay` broker and only under `${clientid}`. Two roles of one broker granting the same filter are reported as a warning; one role's filter covering another role's is named in the warning too. The gateway's publish match (`mqtt::topics::matches`) lives beside it, so the filter grammar the manifest is judged by is the one a publish is decided by.
    - **Explain.** `GET /api/v1/projects/{project}/mqttbrokers/{name}/access?account=<serviceAccount>` answers the roles, filters and validity that `roles_held` gives for that account now, to a person who may read the broker; the broker page shows it.
    - **Deprecation.** A manifest with `serviceAccounts` still works for one release: the reconciler logs a deprecation naming the broker and the role, and `jcctl migrate` writes one `mqtt/<broker>/<role>` binding per listed account into that account's ServiceAccount and drops the list. Access during the migration is the union of both, never more.
    - **Not now.** A reusable project-wide role definition (an `MqttProfile` kind) waits until a project declares the same role on three or more brokers; `mqtt/*/<role>` covers reusing the binding until then.

14. **Allowed source networks.** `spec.allowedSources` lists at most 32 IPv4 or IPv6 networks in CIDR notation; absent or empty admits any address. A municipality uses it to keep its sensors' broker to the city APN and its office ranges. The Portal stamps a Traefik `MiddlewareTCP` `<release>-sources` with `ipAllowList.sourceRange` beside the broker's objects and names it on the broker's route on 8883 and on its dedicated-port route, so Traefik closes a connection from any other address before TLS; the middleware is the broker's own, and no other broker's route names it. Traefik sees the device's own address because the edge Service keeps the client address (`externalTrafficPolicy: Local`). The gateway's CONNECT check refuses a device whose PROXY-protocol address lies outside the list too, as the second line and to record the refusal (`source_address`) where the Portal shows it. The pipeline runner and the Portal's capture read on the internal listener and are not judged by the list. A Hetzner firewall rule cannot tell two brokers on 8883 apart, and a NetworkPolicy `ipBlock` sees Traefik's address after the hop, so neither does this job (PF-129, 2026-10-27).

## 3. Security Analysis

The build tasks (T-3837…T-3839) inherit each row as a failing test.

| Threat | Control |
|---|---|
| A stolen camera token | The token's lifetime bounds a stolen copy: it is worth one connection until `exp`, and the broker drops it at `exp` (`disconnect_if_expiry`). Disabling the ServiceAccount's client in Keycloak stops the next connection. Short access-token lifetimes for camera clients come from the realm (5 minutes by default). |
| Topic squatting | A device's ACL is its role's filters with `${clientid}` bound to its verified `azp`; the gateway refuses a CONNECT whose username or client id differs from `azp`. Every `publish` filter holds `${clientid}` before any wildcard unless the role sets `sharedTopics: true` in a red-lane change. One camera cannot publish under another's topic. |
| A pipeline of one project reading another project's broker | A broker admits the runner only under its own project's name, `pipeline-runner-{project}`, which only a `brokerRef` of that project renders; a DataSource or Pipeline that names a platform broker by URL or a `pipeline-runner…` username is refused (T-5561). |
| A token for another broker or service | The audience must hold `mqtt-{project}-{name}` of the broker asking, and the gateway knows which broker asks from its mesh identity. A gateway or Endpoint token is refused here. |
| Floods | Per-listener `max_connections`, `max_packet_size` and `mqueue_rate_limit` from `spec.limits`, QoS at most 1, no retain, no persistent session; the publish check counts each client's publishes against `messagesPerSecond` and refuses the first one over it, which disconnects the client, so a device costs the gateway at most its rate in publish calls plus one CONNECT check per reconnect (T-5562). Traefik ends TLS on 8883 with the BSI TR-02102-2 TLSOption `mqtt-bsi-tr-02102` of the brokers' namespace, the HTTP edge's settings (T-5562). RMQTT's rustls on a dedicated port offers TLS 1.2 and 1.3 with ECDHE and AEAD suites only: the TLSOption's four TLS 1.2 suites plus the two ECDHE ChaCha20-Poly1305 ones, and X25519 beside the NIST curves, which rustls has no setting to drop; no TLS 1.0 or 1.1, no CBC, no static RSA key exchange (T-5856). Limits have platform ceilings the manifest cannot pass. |
| The auth service is down | `deny_if_error = true` and the single `["deny", "all"]` rule: every CONNECT is refused, and connections that are already open stay up until `exp`. |
| An outside client or a cluster pod posing as the runner | A CONNECT without a password is allowed only for the runner's name from the address of a runner pod. Traffic to the public listeners carries its source address through the PROXY protocol, which Traefik writes on 8883 and on every dedicated port; each of those entrypoints is in Traefik's `config.linkerd.io/skip-inbound-ports`, or Traefik would read every client as 127.0.0.1. The public listener of a broker, 1884 or on a dedicated port 8884, admits Traefik alone. The internal listener admits the runner's mesh identity alone. |
| Plain text from outside | TLS only from outside: Traefik terminates TLS on 8883, and RMQTT on a dedicated port, whose entrypoint `mqtt-<port>` passes TLS through; plain 1883 and 1884 are ClusterIP only. |
| The broker's HTTP API, which can publish and disconnect | It runs only under the mesh and admits one identity, the Portal's, on two read routes (`HTTPRoute` with `GET` only); a NetworkPolicy lets no other pod reach 6060. It carries no static token: the mesh identity is the credential, as on 1883. |
| A secret typed into the manifest | The schema has no field that could hold a credential; the broker uses none, and a DataSource with `brokerRef` refuses `passwordRef`. The allowance's `certificateRef` and `clientCaRef` are `secretRef`s only. |
| A project member raising its own allowance, picking a port or a hostname | `spec.quotas.mqtt` and `spec.limits.mqtt` are written only through a red-lane Change an `org-admin` approves, and a raise needs a second org admin (item 9). A broker may name only a port or hostname its project's allowance holds (item 11). |
| MQTT on in a project nobody decided | The catalog default is `enabled: false`; the verdict and the reconciler refuse every broker of a project whose allowance is off. |
| Captured payloads holding personal data | A capture lives at most `window` (at most 24 hours) and at most 1000 messages and 8 MiB per broker, is deleted with the mode or the broker, is served only to a person with `approve` on `MqttBroker` in the project, and never reaches a log, an activity entry or a metric (item 10). |
| A device with a stolen client certificate | The CA is the project's own, so a certificate is worth one project; the CRL in the CA secret and the role binding refuse its next CONNECT, every connection is checked again within an hour, and the CN must match the client id (item 12). |

## 4. Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| RMQTT's `rmqtt-auth-jwt` with the realm key as a PEM | It reads one static key: a Keycloak key rotation breaks every device until a re-render. It also cannot bind the ACL to a ServiceAccount's role without carrying the ACL in the token. The gateway already verifies Keycloak tokens against JWKS. |
| The ACL as an `acl` claim minted into each token | Puts authorization in Keycloak mappers per client and per broker, invisible in the project's Git repository. |
| rumqttd | No published image, no HTTP or JWT authentication, last release more than a year older; kept as the fallback should RMQTT fail its build task. |
| A broker of our own | The owner asked for an existing one. |
| The pipeline authenticated by a token in the password | Bento's `mqtt` input reads the password once, so the first reconnect after `exp` fails. A refreshing stream would need a forked runner. |
| The pipeline authenticated by its client secret | A static key between cluster services, which the platform does not use. |
| One wildcard certificate for `*.mqtt.<domain>` | Needs a DNS API; certificates are issued per hostname by HTTP-01. |
| Every broker approved by an admin, with no allowance (amendment) | An admin in the loop for every broker a project adds. Kept only for raising an allowance, where a second org admin approves (item 9). |
| An admin-made address object assigned to a project, which users attach brokers to (amendment) | A second kind for what the allowance's `ports` and `customHostnames` already say; the address part of the allowance is that object. |
| One shared platform broker, a topic prefix per project (amendment) | One RMQTT for every project breaks the per-broker audience and client isolation of item 6. |
| A bring-your-own external broker as a DataSource (amendment) | It stays, behind the existing host allow-list (MF-35, PL-39); it is not an edge broker of the platform. |

## 5. Consequences

- **`jc-core`** gains `MqttBroker` (MF-54) and `DataSource.spec.mqtt.brokerRef`; `jcctl` places the kind, waves it after ServiceAccounts, and validates roles, limits and the port range.
- **The gateway** serves `POST /internal/mqtt/auth` and `POST /internal/mqtt/acl` for broker pods only (PL-75).
- **The reconciler** renders each broker's release, Keycloak client and Traefik route, refuses a duplicate port, and resolves `brokerRef` (T-3837).
- **The deployment** mirrors and signs the RMQTT image, adds the `{instance}-mqtt` namespace, the Traefik `mqtts` entrypoint on 8883, and the NetworkPolicies and Linkerd policies of section 2 (T-3837).
- **The Portal** lists, creates and edits brokers as Changes and shows each broker's address and a copy-ready client configuration (T-3838).
- **The camera demo** publishes through a broker into a space (T-3839).
- **The amendment (items 9 to 12)**: jc-core gains `limits.mqtt` and `quotas.mqtt`, and `MqttBroker` gains `mode`, `capture`, `address`, `hostname` and `deviceAuth`; the Portal's verdict and the reconciler enforce the allowance, the gateway admits the Portal's capture subscriber and certificate devices, and the reconciler renders custom hostnames and client CAs (T-4859, T-4861). The Portal shows the allowance in Organization settings and on the project's MQTT page, and the capture to stewards (T-4860). A broker committed before the amendment with device `subscribe` filters needs `mode: relay`; one without them becomes `ingest` and behaves as before.
